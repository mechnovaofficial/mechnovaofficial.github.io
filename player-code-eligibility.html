New player or returning player? Which Mech Arena codes fit your account

By Krushna Zanak Narhe · 27 November 2026

A friend sends you a clip of a successful claim. You copy the same characters on the same day and get an eligibility error. Both results can be genuine. The clip shows what happened to one player. To decide whether to spend an attempt, you need three details that often sit outside the frame: which account the player used, when the offer applied, and whether the reward came from a code or a sign-up link.

Start with the account that will receive the reward

Open the hangar you intend to keep. If it contains your old mechs and progress after a reinstall, you are looking at an existing account. Installing the app on a new phone tells you when that device downloaded the game. It tells you very little about when the account was created. If your old hangar is unexpectedly empty, resolve the sign-in issue before claiming anything. A successful claim on the wrong profile leaves you with a second problem.

Plarium describes beginner promotions, promotions for experienced players coming back, and time-limited codes. MechNova uses these distinctions to organise the directory. An account category answers “who can apply?” A campaign deadline answers “when can they apply?” A beginner offer can have both restrictions. An “all players” entry can still close while its announcement and thousands of shared screenshots remain online.

If this is your first account

Find the original campaign wording before trying several starter codes in succession. Look for the event that starts the clock. A campaign may refer to account creation, while a pack may ask you to begin through a particular link. If the text gives you a number of hours from creation, count from when you made the account. Completing the tutorial later or opening a creator's video today doesn't establish a later start.

For example, a moderator's November code list on the Mech Arena forum described a 24-hour window and a choice of one starter code from the options in that post. Treat that as a condition of those listed offers. Check today's announcement before applying it to a different batch. If you already used one from a mutually exclusive group, another spelling or another device won't restore that choice. Read the rewards side by side before making the first claim.

Compare what you can actually equip. Suppose one starter choice advertises a weapon and another advertises a mech. Open your hangar and inspect the weapon's energy requirement and the mech you plan to use. A weapon you cannot fit yet is a future upgrade project. A mech that fills an empty role might help immediately. If the offer shows a skin, read the item label before counting the pictured mech as part of the reward.

If you are coming back to an old hangar

Read the offer addressed to the account you recovered. Check for a stated absence requirement, a claim deadline after returning, and any instructions specific to the invitation. The time you spent away and the time since you came back are different measurements. A long break may satisfy one condition while a late attempt misses the other. If the publisher hasn't supplied a threshold, keep that detail unknown.

Consider two friends who both last played in the same month. One sees a welcome-back offer in the Inbox and the other sees a public News post. Compare the reward descriptions and the route each player is asked to follow. One invitation might have a claim button, while the public post describes a code. Typing the invitation's headline into the promo field won't test either friend's eligibility. Follow each offer from its own instructions.

If you play regularly

Start in the all-player category and check whether the listing carries an end date or a dated test. A dated redemption is stronger evidence than an undated repost, but a one-time claim is difficult to retest on the same account. An “already redeemed” result from the original player therefore tells a different story from a successful first claim made today. Keep those observations separate when deciding what to try.

That distinction matters on this site. My October 30 TRICKORFEAR video described 200 A-Coins, 31,100 Credits and 13 Fortune Keys. A player finding the video weeks later can use it to understand the contents of that claim. The date and item amounts do not establish that the campaign is still available. Keys are also the reward in their own right. Anything obtained by spending them is a separate outcome.

When a starter pack appears next to a code

Read the button before leaving the directory. “Copy” gives you text to enter. A starter-pack link takes you to offer terms, which may specify a registration route, platform or purchase. Check the recipient account, the price if any and the items explicitly included. If you already play and the offer requires a new registration, opening the link alone does not establish that your existing hangar qualifies. Keep your current account intact while checking the terms.

What to send if the category looks wrong

Send the page URL, category on the card, date of the attempt and exact non-sensitive message. Mention whether you created an account, returned to an existing one or played continuously. If the announcement gives a different condition, include that source. This gives me enough context to correct the listing without guessing from a single rejection. For an account-specific decision, use Plarium Support. Only the game can determine eligibility for your account.
